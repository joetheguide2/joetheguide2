Fallacy Benchmarks Measure Scheme Recognition, Not Fallacy Detection

Preprint, 2026

Fallacy-detection benchmarks report very low false-positive rates, which is usually read as evidence that models rarely over-flag valid arguments. This paper shows that the number is small because of how the "valid" class is assembled in the first place: it isn't built the way the fallacy classes are, but is instead the leftover material from an annotation process — off-topic remarks, bare opinions, fragments, and genuine arguments that just don't share a fallacy's reasoning pattern. Sampling the valid class across four benchmarks turns up almost no material that is actually scheme-matched to the fallacies being tested, meaning a model can score well by recognizing which argumentation scheme is in play rather than by telling valid reasoning apart from fallacious reasoning.

NSIDDx: A Design Framework for Neuro-Symbolic, Practitioner-First Differential Diagnosis in Low-Resource Settings

NLP4PI Workshop, EMNLP 2026 — Accepted

LLM-based diagnostic systems post high semantic accuracy on benchmarks, but open-ended evaluation on clinically uncommon cases reveals a wide gap between that headline accuracy and outputs a clinician can actually verify. Evaluating an LLM plus rare-disease RAG pipeline across two cohorts shows the approach produces confident answers that are often unverifiable and resistant to follow-up questioning. NSIDDx is proposed as a design framework arguing that diagnostic systems in low-resource settings need to treat the clinician as an active reasoning partner rather than a rubber stamp, instantiated as a neuro-symbolic pipeline — ternary symptom encoding, contradiction detection, audit strings, and practitioner override — that runs fully offline on consumer hardware.
